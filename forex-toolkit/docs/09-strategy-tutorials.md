# Strategy Tutorials — Plain English, Step by Step

Nine strategies, each explained the way you'd explain them to a friend: what
it actually is, when to trade it in Philippine time, the steps, a worked
example with real numbers, and the mistakes that cost beginners money. The
same tutorials are built into the app (Guide → Strategy tutorials).

For every one of them: practice on demo first, log every attempt in the
Journal with account set to **Demo**, and let the Roadmap's gate tell you
when it's actually working. Full technical rule-sets are in
`03-strategies.md`, `06-advanced-strategies.md` and
`08-smart-money-concepts.md`.

## 1. Pullback trend-following — Beginner
**When:** flexible; best 15:00–18:00 or 20:30–24:00 PHT · **Pairs:** any major

**What it is.** Markets that trend don't move in straight lines — they take
two steps forward, one step back. This strategy buys the "step back" in an
uptrend (or sells it in a downtrend), so you enter at a discount just as the
trend gets going again. Recommended first strategy: few trades, forgiving
entries, high reward for the risk.

**You need:** any charting app (TradingView free is fine) with a 50 EMA on
the Daily, 4-hour and 1-hour charts.

**Steps**
1. Find the trend. On the Daily AND 4-hour chart, price must be above a
   rising 50 EMA — uptrend, buys only. Below a falling 50 EMA on both —
   sells only. If they disagree, skip the pair today.
2. Wait for the pullback: price dips to the 20–50 EMA zone or to a level
   that used to be resistance (old ceilings become new floors).
3. Wait for the signal: on the 1-hour, a bullish engulfing or pin bar
   rejecting the zone. No signal = no trade, however perfect the zone looks.
4. Place the trade: stop a few pips below the pullback's low; size with the
   Position Size calculator at 0.5% risk.
5. Target the recent high or 2× your risk, whichever is nearer. Walk away.

**Worked example.** EURUSD Daily and H4 above a rising 50 EMA. Price pulls
back from 1.0920 to 1.0860 — the H4 50 EMA plus an old resistance line. An
H1 bullish engulfing prints. Buy 1.0865, stop 1.0840 (25 pips), target
1.0915 (2R). $100,000 account × 0.5% = $500 risk → 2.0 lots.

**Mistakes to avoid:** entering at a zone with no signal candle; trading
against the Daily trend; moving the stop away from price.

**Checklist:** Daily + H4 agree · pullback into zone · H1 rejection candle ·
stop beyond swing at 0.5% · target ≥ 1.5R.

## 2. London breakout — Beginner
**When:** 14:30–18:00 PHT · **Pairs:** EURUSD, GBPUSD

**What it is.** During the quiet Tokyo hours the pair builds a tight range.
When London wakes up — 3 PM your time — the first big push often breaks the
range and keeps running. You trade that push, but only after it proves
itself with a close and a retest.

**You need:** a 15m/30m chart, free time 14:30–18:00 PHT.

**Steps**
1. Around 14:30 PHT, mark the overnight high and low (roughly the last 7
   hours). Skip if the range is over ~50 pips or red news lands before
   18:00 PHT.
2. Wait for a candle to **close** outside the range — a wick doesn't count.
3. Don't chase. Wait for the retest — price returning to the broken line —
   then enter in the breakout direction.
4. Stop on the other side of the retest swing (10–20 pips). Target 1.5–2R
   or yesterday's high/low.
5. Nothing by 19:00 PHT? Done. The edge is the opening burst.

**Worked example.** Range 1.0850–1.0872 (22 pips). At 15:30 PHT a 15m candle
closes at 1.0880. Price dips to 1.0872 — buy the retest, stop 1.0860
(12 pips), target 1.0896 (2R). Over by 16:30.

**Mistakes:** buying the first poke-out candle; flipping direction after a
failed break; trading wide-range days.

**Checklist:** range marked before 15:00 · 15–50 pips tall · closed outside ·
retest entry · no red news before 18:00 PHT.

## 3. Range trading at extremes — Beginner+
**When:** quiet periods (Tokyo hours, between news cycles) · **Pairs:** EURGBP, AUDNZD, quiet majors

**What it is.** When a pair is stuck between a floor and a ceiling for days,
you can literally buy low and sell high: fade the edges, profit in the
middle. The skill is knowing when the range is real — and when it's dying.

**You need:** H1/H4 charts; a pair clearly sideways for a week+.

**Steps**
1. Qualify: two clean bounces off BOTH edges minimum, at least ~40 pips tall.
2. Price touches an edge → wait for a rejection candle there (H1 pin/engulfing).
3. Enter toward the middle; stop just past the edge, beyond the most recent
   fake-out wick.
4. Target mid-range (safe) or far side (ambitious); half at mid is a good
   compromise.
5. Kill switch: any H4 **close** outside the range kills it. Flatten,
   delete orders, stand aside.

**Worked example.** EURGBP between 0.8420 and 0.8480 for two weeks. Touch of
0.8480 prints an H1 pin bar. Sell 0.8474, stop 0.8492 (18 pips), target
0.8450 (~1.3R), runner to 0.8430.

**Mistakes:** fading without a rejection signal; trading ranges into big
news; holding after an H4 close outside.

**Checklist:** 2+ touches both edges · ≥40 pips · rejection candle · stop
past edge · no imminent news.

## 4. Higher-timeframe swing — Beginner (slow)
**When:** 20–30 min any evening; trades last days–weeks · **Pairs:** majors + EURJPY/GBPJPY

**What it is.** The slowest, calmest strategy: daily charts only, twenty
minutes an evening. It's the pullback strategy one level up — daily entries
at weekly levels. Perfect around a day job.

**You need:** Daily + Weekly charts. Mostly patience.

**Steps**
1. Each evening, read the Weekly: trend direction, plus the 2–3 levels
   obvious from across the room.
2. Wait for a pullback to one of those levels in the trend direction (can
   take days — that's the design).
3. Wait for a Daily rejection candle at the level.
4. Enter next day; stop past the signal candle's extreme (40–80 pips is
   normal — the calculator just gives you fewer lots).
5. Target the next weekly level, ≥2R. Check once a day, never once an hour.

**Worked example.** GBPUSD weekly uptrend pulls back to 1.2600 weekly
support; a daily pin bar rejects. Buy 1.2650, stop 1.2570 (80 pips), target
1.2810 (2R). $500 risk → 0.62 lots. Nine days to target.

**Mistakes:** hourly chart-checking and panic exits; holding weekends
against firm rules; forgetting that wide stops mean fewer lots.

**Checklist:** weekly trend clear · daily signal AT weekly level · stop past
candle · size recomputed · weekend rule checked.

## 5. Break and retest — Intermediate
**When:** London or New York hours · **Pairs:** any major

**What it is.** When an important level finally breaks, price very often
returns to "kiss it goodbye" before the real move — the old ceiling becomes
the new floor. You leave a limit order at the kiss instead of chasing the
break: better price, better proof, calmer entry.

**You need:** H1–H4 charts; the patience to sometimes miss the move.

**Steps**
1. Find a level respected at least twice — the more obvious, the better.
2. Wait for a full-bodied H1/H4 **close** through it.
3. Don't chase: limit order at the broken level.
4. Stop past the small post-break swing; target the next level, only if
   ≥2R away.
5. No retest = no trade. Missing non-setups is the strategy working.

**Worked example.** USDJPY bounced off 150.00 three times; H4 closes at
150.35. Limit buy 150.05 fills next session; stop 149.70 (35 pips), target
150.75 (2R).

**Mistakes:** entering on the breakout candle; counting a wick as a break;
holding through a retest that closes back through (failed break — exit).

**Checklist:** level touched 2+ · full close through · limit at level ·
stop past new swing · next level ≥ 2R.

## 6. MA crossover baseline — Beginner (mechanical)
**When:** check once per H4 candle close · **Pairs:** 2–3 trending majors

**What it is.** Fully mechanical, zero judgment — the training-wheels
strategy. You won't get rich from it, but you'll learn flawless execution,
and it becomes the benchmark your discretionary trading must beat.

**You need:** H4 with 20 + 50 EMA; Daily with 200 EMA; ADX (optional filter).

**Steps**
1. Daily 200 EMA filter: above + rising → longs only; below + falling →
   shorts only.
2. H4 20 EMA crosses above the 50 → enter on the crossover candle's close.
3. Stop below the last H4 swing low. Skip if ADX < 20.
4. Exit at 2R **or** the opposite cross — pick ONE rule before starting,
   never switch mid-test.
5. Take EVERY signal for 30 trades. Its whole edge is never missing the big
   trend — which requires never cherry-picking.

**Worked example.** AUDUSD above rising Daily 200 EMA; H4 20 crosses above
50 with ADX 24. Enter 0.6620, stop 0.6585 (35 pips), exit 2R at 0.6690.

**Mistakes:** skipping signals that "feel wrong"; no ADX filter in chop;
changing exits after losses.

**Checklist:** 200 EMA filter · crossover close · ADX > 20 · one exit rule ·
every signal taken.

## 7. ICT 4-step liquidity sweep — Advanced
**When:** killzones only — 14:00–17:00 or 20:30–23:30 PHT · **Pairs:** EURUSD, GBPUSD, GBPJPY

**What it is.** The prop-firm favorite. Big players need your stop-losses to
fill their orders, so price often spikes past an obvious high/low — grabbing
everyone's stops — then reverses hard. You wait for the trap to spring,
demand confirmation, and ride the reversal with a tiny stop.

**You need:** 15m + 5m + 1m charts, killzone availability, and the SMC guide
read at least twice. Demo until the pattern is obvious.

**Steps**
1. Pre-session, draw the liquidity map: yesterday's high/low, Asia/London
   session highs/lows, equal highs/lows. Triggers AND targets.
2. In the killzone, wait for a spike THROUGH a mapped level that **closes
   back inside** — a long wick through it. The trap.
3. Confirm on the 5m: break of structure or inverse fair value gap in the
   new direction. No confirmation, no trade.
4. Wait for the pullback (a small 1m push against the new direction, into
   the fresh gap or ~50% of the impulse).
5. Enter on the 1m break of structure back in your direction. Stop past the
   spike's wick; target the OPPOSITE mapped level; half off at the first
   pool, stop to breakeven.

**Worked example.** 20:35 PHT: GBPUSD spikes 8 pips above yesterday's high
1.2740, closes back below. 5m breaks structure down at 1.2725. 1m pulls back
to 1.2733, breaks down again → sell 1.2731, stop 1.2747 (16 pips), first
target Asian low 1.2700 (~2R), runner to the London low.

**Mistakes:** entering on the spike pre-confirmation; trading outside
killzones; fading sweeps on strong trend days (there they're continuation).

**Checklist:** map drawn pre-session · sweep closes back inside · 5m
BOS/IFVG · 1m trigger after pullback · stop past wick, target opposite pool.

## 8. ICT Silver Bullet — Intermediate+
**When:** one fixed hour — 22:00–23:00 PHT (23:00–24:00 Nov–Mar) · **Pairs:** EURUSD, GBPUSD, indices

**What it is.** The most structured ICT setup: one fixed hour, one pattern,
in or out. Find the first fair value gap pointing toward the level price is
already reaching for, ride it there. No setup in the hour = no trade today.
The window IS the strategy. Very day-job friendly.

**Steps**
1. Before 22:00 PHT, answer one question: which obvious level is price
   reaching for? Write it down — that's the target.
2. From 22:00, watch for a strong 3-candle burst toward it that leaves a
   gap (candle 1's wick and candle 3's wick don't touch).
3. Limit order at the gap's near edge; stop past the swing that made it.
4. Take it only if the target is ≥2R away.
5. 23:00, no setup? Platform closed, "no trade" journaled. Zero-trade days
   are wins on a funded account.

**Worked example.** Target: yesterday's low 1.0800; price 1.0830 at the
open. 22:15 PHT: a strong 5m drop leaves a gap 1.0824–1.0819. Limit sell
1.0822, stop 1.0832 (10 pips), target 1.0801 ≈ 2.1R. Filled 22:30, target
hit before midnight.

**Mistakes:** forcing a weak gap as the hour ends; gaps against the draw;
accepting < 2R.

**Checklist:** target chosen pre-window · gap inside window toward target ·
limit at gap edge · ≥2R · no setup = no trade.

## 9. ICT London Judas swing — Advanced
**When:** London open — 14:00–16:00 PHT · **Pairs:** EURUSD, GBPUSD

**What it is.** London's famous head-fake. The first move at the open often
runs the WRONG way — just far enough to grab the stops beyond the overnight
range — before the day's real move launches opposite. You skip the fake and
board the real one.

**Steps**
1. Mark the Asian range (≈ 08:00–14:00 PHT) high and low.
2. At the open, watch the first push. Spike through one side + stall (long
   wicks, no follow-through) = possible Judas.
3. Wait for a FAST, full-bodied move back through the range that breaks 5m
   structure — displacement. A slow drift back doesn't count.
4. Enter on the pullback into the gap the fast move left; stop past the
   fake-out's extreme.
5. Target the far side of the Asian range, then yesterday's high/low.

**Worked example.** Range 1.0840–1.0862. 14:10 PHT: spike to 1.0871, stall.
14:40: powerful drop back through 1.0850 breaking structure, gap at
1.0855–1.0851. Sell the pullback 1.0853, stop 1.0873 (20 pips), target
1.0820 beyond the range low.

**Mistakes:** treating every open spike as fake (without displacement you
may be shorting a real breakout); trading UK/EU red-news mornings; entering
before the confirmation move.

**Checklist:** Asian range marked · spike + stall · fast displacement back ·
gap pullback entry · stop past the fake-out wick.
