# Trading Psychology & Discipline

A profitable strategy executed badly loses money; a mediocre strategy executed
perfectly roughly breaks even. On a funded account — where one undisciplined
afternoon breaches a limit that took weeks to earn — psychology *is* the job.

## 1. Why trading breaks normal decision-making

Trading gives you randomized rewards (any single trade is close to a coin
flip), real money consequences, instant feedback, and a button that lets you
act on any impulse within seconds. That combination reliably produces the same
failure patterns in almost everyone. You don't fix them with willpower; you fix
them with **rules decided in advance and made mechanical**.

## 2. The cognitive biases that cost the most

| Bias | How it shows up | The mechanical fix |
|---|---|---|
| Loss aversion | Widening/removing stops; closing winners at +0.3R "before they turn" | Hard stop placed with the order; targets pre-set; never touch either against your plan |
| Revenge trading | Instant oversized re-entry after a loss | Two-loss / −1.5% daily circuit breaker; 10-minute cooldown after any loss |
| FOMO | Chasing a candle that already ran 40 pips | Entries only at pre-marked levels via limit/stop orders; if it's gone, it's gone |
| Recency bias | Ditching a system after 3 losses; over-trusting it after 3 wins | Judge only 30-trade blocks; the journal's expectancy line, nothing else |
| Overconfidence | Doubling risk during a winning streak | Risk % is a constant, changed only at monthly review — never intraday |
| Sunk-cost | Averaging into losers ("it owes me") | Adding to a losing position is banned, full stop. Martingale is how accounts die |
| Confirmation bias | Only seeing evidence for your open position | Write the invalidation ("wrong if X") in the plan before entry; X happens → out |
| Outcome bias | Calling a reckless win "good trading" | Grade every trade A–D on rule-following, not on profit |

## 3. Tilt — recognize it in real time

Tilt is decision-making hijacked by frustration. On-ramp symptoms, in order:

1. Checking P/L every few seconds instead of the chart
2. Shrinking timeframes (H1 trader suddenly staring at M1)
3. "It has to bounce here" reasoning — narrative replacing levels
4. Size creeping up mid-session
5. The revenge entry

**Protocol:** at symptom 1–2, stand up for ten minutes. At 3+, or after two
losses, or at −1.5% on the day — platform closed, session over, journal
written. The rule works because it's absolute; a rule with a "unless it's a
really good setup" clause is not a rule.

## 4. Process over outcome — the only sane scoreboard

Any single trade result is noise. The scoreboard that converges to your skill:

- **A-grade trade:** followed plan entirely (entry, size, stop, exit). Win or
  lose, this is a success.
- **D-grade trade:** broke a rule. A D that made money is the most expensive
  kind — it trains the exact behavior that later breaches the account.

Track the A-rate in your journal notes. Traders with a 90% A-rate and a
mediocre strategy routinely outlast brilliant analysts with a 50% A-rate.

## 5. Daily structure beats daily willpower

- **Pre-session (10 min):** update the Funded Tracker; check the calendar;
  mark levels; write the day's if-then scenarios ("if EURUSD retests 1.0850
  with rejection, I take the long"). No scenario → no trade today, and that's
  a completed session, not a wasted one.
- **In-session:** execute only what was written. New idea mid-session? Note it
  for tomorrow's plan. Live improvisation is where D-grades come from.
- **Post-session (5 min):** journal each trade in R with one honest sentence
  and a letter grade. Close with tomorrow's single focus point.
- **Weekly (30 min):** expectancy trend, A-rate, rule breaches, one thing to
  change. Change at most one variable per week.

## 6. Handling the two dangerous emotional states

**After a losing streak:** cut risk to 0.25% until two consecutive A-grade
wins. The goal is repairing execution confidence, not the balance — the
balance follows.

**After a winning streak / near the profit target:** this is statistically
where accounts blow up, because arousal is high and caution feels unnecessary.
Milestone throttle: at 75% of target, halve risk. Passing one day later costs
nothing; round-tripping +6% to a breach costs the fee and the month.

## 7. Life support for the machine that trades

Unglamorous, decisive, and all free:

- **Sleep** — one bad night measurably degrades risk decisions; don't trade
  the London open on five hours.
- **No alcohol/session overlap**, obviously — but also no trading during
  emotionally loaded life events (fights, job stress, financial pressure).
  The market can't tell you're compromised; your size can't either.
- **Money pressure ruins execution.** Trading rent money makes every trade
  feel life-sized, which guarantees loss-averse mistakes. Fund evaluations
  only from truly disposable income.
- **One screen, one strategy, few pairs.** Attention is capital. Twelve
  watchlists is not an edge, it's a slot machine.

## 8. The meta-skill

The evaluation isn't testing chart-reading — charts are the easy 20%. It's
testing whether you can follow your own written rules for thirty consecutive
sessions while variance actively provokes you. That skill is trainable, it
transfers to the funded account unchanged, and every tool in this kit (the
tracker's suggested risk, the planner's checklist, the journal's grades) exists
to train it.
