Guide & strategies

A working reference: the mechanics, the risk rules that keep accounts alive, strategies from core through advanced to the smart-money (ICT) models prop traders favor, the psychology that decides whether any of it holds, a playbook for passing funded-account evaluations, and a glossary.

How forex trading actually works

You trade one currency against another. Buying EURUSD at 1.0850 means you profit if the euro strengthens against the dollar. Prices move in pips — 0.0001 for most pairs, 0.01 for JPY pairs. A standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency; one pip on one lot of a USD-quoted pair is worth $10. Mini lots (0.1) and micro lots (0.01) scale that down to $1 and $0.10.

Sessions matter more than beginners think

  • Tokyo (00:00–09:00 UTC ≈ 08:00–17:00 PHT) — quieter, range-bound; JPY and AUD pairs most active. Your morning.
  • London (07:00–16:00 UTC ≈ 15:00–24:00 PHT) — the largest session; most trends start here. Your afternoon/evening.
  • New York (13:00–22:00 UTC ≈ 21:00–06:00 PHT) — USD news lands here; the London/NY overlap (≈ 21:00–24:00 PHT) has the deepest liquidity and cleanest moves of the day. Your evening — the most convenient prime window from the Philippines.

PHT times shown for UK/US summer; add one hour November–March (Manila has no daylight saving). The Market clock on the Funded Tracker tab always shows the exact current conversion.

Spread, slippage and news

The spread is your cost per trade; it widens violently around high-impact news (NFP, CPI, FOMC) and at rollover (~21:00–22:00 UTC). Prop firms often prohibit or penalize holding through major news — check the rules. Keep an economic calendar open every session.

Reading a chart, minimally

You need surprisingly little: the trend on the daily and H4 (higher highs/lows or lower highs/lows), the obvious horizontal levels where price reversed before, and one confirmation tool you trust (a moving average pair, or plain candlestick signals like engulfing bars at a level). Everything else is decoration until your journal proves otherwise.

Full written guides — with more depth than fits here — are in the docs/ folder of this toolkit.

Risk rules that keep accounts alive

Nearly every blown evaluation dies the same way: normal losing streaks meet oversized positions. The math is unforgiving — a 10% drawdown needs 11.1% to recover; a 50% drawdown needs 100%.

The core rules

  1. Fixed fractional risk: risk the same small % on every trade — 0.5% is the sweet spot for evaluations, 1% the absolute ceiling. Size the position from the stop, never the stop from the position.
  2. A daily stop: after 2 losses or −1.5% on the day, you're done. Losing streaks and tilt compound; tomorrow's spread is the same price.
  3. Minimum 1.5R reward: with 2R targets you're profitable winning only 34% of trades. The breakeven win rate is 1 ÷ (1 + R:R).
  4. One correlated exposure at a time: long EURUSD + long GBPUSD + short USDCHF is one trade wearing three costumes. Count correlated positions as a single risk unit.
  5. Never move a stop away from price. Widen the stop and you've silently multiplied your risk. Move stops only toward profit.

Why 0.5% risk wins evaluations

At 0.5% risk, a 5-trade losing streak — which will happen, even with a 55% win rate — costs ~2.5%, well inside a 5% daily / 10% overall limit. At 2% risk the same streak costs ~10% and the account is gone. You cannot pass a challenge you've already breached.

Expectancy beats win rate. Expectancy = (win% × avg win) − (loss% × avg loss). A 40% win rate with 2R winners earns 0.2R per trade; a 70% win rate with 0.3R winners and 1R losers loses money. Your journal tab computes this for you.

Four concrete strategies

Pick one, trade it for 30+ journaled trades on a demo or small account, and only keep it if expectancy is positive. Rules below are starting points to refine — not guarantees.

1. London breakout (session momentum)

  • Mark the high/low of the Asian range (00:00–07:00 UTC) on EURUSD or GBPUSD.
  • From 07:00–10:00 UTC, trade a break and close beyond the range on M15/M30 — ideally with a retest of the broken level.
  • Stop: other side of the retest swing, or mid-range. Target: 1.5–2R, or the previous day's high/low.
  • Skip days where the Asian range is unusually wide or major news lands at the London open.

2. Pullback trend-following (the evaluation workhorse)

  • Trend filter: daily and H4 agree (e.g. price above a rising 50 EMA on both) — longs only in uptrends, shorts only in downtrends.
  • Wait for a pullback into the 20–50 EMA zone or a prior structure level on H1/H4.
  • Enter on a confirmation candle (engulfing / pin bar rejecting the level). Stop below the pullback swing. Target the recent high or a fixed 2R.
  • Strength: high R:R, low screen time, few trades — exactly what drawdown-limited accounts want.

3. Range trading at session extremes

  • Instrument: pairs in a well-defined multi-day range (EURGBP, AUDNZD, or majors between news cycles).
  • Fade touches of range extremes with a rejection signal — never catch a knife into a fresh breakout.
  • Stop just beyond the extreme; target mid-range (conservative) or the far side (aggressive). Stand aside when a breakout closes outside the range.

4. Higher-timeframe swing (for small time budgets)

  • Daily charts only; 20–30 minutes per evening. Trade pullbacks to weekly/daily levels in the direction of the weekly trend.
  • Wider stops (40–80 pips) mean smaller position sizes — the position-size calculator handles this automatically.
  • Mind weekend gaps and swap costs on held positions; check whether your funded account permits holding over the weekend.
Whatever you choose: one strategy, one or two pairs, 30 trades, journal everything. Strategy-hopping after three losses is the most reliable way to never find out if anything works.

Advanced strategies

These need more chart-reading skill than the core four. Same rules apply: one at a time, 30+ journaled trades, judge by expectancy. Full write-ups in docs/06-advanced-strategies.md.

5. Break and retest

  • Identify a level the market has respected at least twice (H1–H4). Wait for a decisive close through it.
  • Do not chase the breakout candle. Place a limit order at the broken level and wait for the retest — old resistance becomes new support (and vice versa).
  • Stop beyond the retest swing; target the next structural level, minimum 2R. If price never retests, you miss the trade — that's the strategy working, not failing.

6. Liquidity sweep reversal (stop-hunt fade)

  • Mark obvious liquidity pools: equal highs/lows, yesterday's high/low, session extremes — clusters where stops accumulate.
  • Setup: price spikes through the pool, then closes back inside the prior range within 1–3 candles (the sweep + reclaim). That failed break traps breakout traders.
  • Enter on the reclaim close or its retest; stop beyond the sweep wick; target the opposite side of the range. Works best during London and NY opens; skip it in strong trends.

7. Fibonacci confluence pullback

  • In a trend, draw the fib retracement over the last impulse leg. Interest zone: 38.2–61.8%.
  • Only act where a fib level stacks with an independent reason — prior structure, a moving average, a round number. One tool alone is a coin flip; confluence is the edge.
  • Trigger, stop and management identical to the core pullback strategy; targets at the −27% / −62% extensions or prior extreme.

8. Divergence reversal at higher-timeframe levels

  • Location first: price at a weekly/daily level, not mid-range. Then look for RSI(14) divergence — price makes a new high/low, RSI doesn't.
  • Confirm with a reversal candle on H4/D1 before entering; divergence alone can persist through 200 more pips of trend.
  • Stop beyond the extreme; first target the nearest H4 structure (~1.5–2R). Counter-trend, so: half normal risk, and never during momentum news days.

9. Moving-average crossover baseline (mechanical)

  • H4: 20 EMA crosses 50 EMA in the direction of the daily 200 EMA slope. Enter on the close of the crossover candle; stop below the last swing; exit on the opposite cross or 2R.
  • Whipsaws sideways markets — add an ADX(14) > 20 filter or skip ranging pairs. Its virtue is zero discretion: useful as a benchmark to measure your discretionary results against.
Trend-following setups (2, 5, 7, 9) forgive imperfect entries; reversal setups (6, 8) do not. Master a trend strategy before touching reversals — and never trade reversals near your daily loss limit.

Smart Money Concepts (ICT) — the liquidity playbook

The strategy school most visible among funded prop traders: instead of indicators, read price through liquidity — the stop orders resting above highs and below lows. Full write-up with all four models in docs/08-smart-money-concepts.md.

The core logic

Old highs hide buy orders (shorts' stops + breakout buyers); old lows hide sell orders. A large seller needs buyers to sell to — so price is routinely pushed through a high to fill those orders, then reverses. Sweeps of liquidity are entry signals (after confirmation); pools of liquidity are your targets.

The 4-step intraday model

  1. Map the draws on liquidity before the session: Asia/London highs & lows, previous day's high/low, equal highs/lows, untaken 1H/4H swings.
  2. Manipulation: during a killzone, price sweeps one of your levels — a wick through it that closes back inside. (A close that holds beyond = breakout, not a sweep. Stand down.)
  3. 5-minute confirmation: a break of structure or an inverse fair value gap in the reversal direction. No confirmation, no trade.
  4. Retrace, then 1-minute trigger: wait for the pullback into the fresh 5m gap or the 50% equilibrium (it shows up as a small 1m structure break against you), then enter on the 1m break of structure back in your direction. Stop beyond the sweep's wick; targets at the opposing liquidity pools; partial at the first pool, stop to breakeven.

The filters that carry the edge

  • Killzones only: London ≈ 07:00–10:00 London time (14:00–17:00 PHT in UK summer, 15:00–18:00 in winter), New York ≈ 08:30–11:30 New York time (20:30–23:30 PHT in US summer, 21:30–00:30 in winter). The Market clock on the tracker tab always shows the current conversion. Nothing in the first ~90 minutes → done for the day.
  • Correlation alignment: the forex version of "ES and NASDAQ must agree" — take EURUSD longs only when GBPUSD (or an inverted DXY) shows the same 5m structure shift. Twins disagreeing = indecision = no trade.
  • One or two trades a day. The model's main job is filtering trades out — on a loss-capped funded account, that's exactly the right shape.

Also in the full doc

  • Silver Bullet — fixed one-hour window (e.g. 14:00–15:00 UTC): first FVG toward the draw, ≥2R only.
  • London Judas swing — the fake-out sweep of the Asian range at London open, entered on displacement back through it.
  • Power of Three — accumulation → manipulation → distribution as a daily bias lens, plus premium/discount.
Keep your skepticism. Screenshots of anyone's P/L — however large — are not your data. Backtest 30+ trades in the Journal tab first; keep 1m stops ≥ ~5× the spread; and remember NY killzone sits on top of US news releases your firm may prohibit trading.

Funded-account playbook

Prop-firm evaluations (Equity Edge, FTMO-style firms, and similar) sell you a shot at trading their capital: pass a profit target without breaching loss limits, and you split profits on a funded account. Typical two-phase structure:

RulePhase 1Phase 2Funded
Profit target8–10%4–5%none
Max daily loss4–5%4–5%4–5%
Max overall drawdown8–10%8–10%8–10%
Profit split——70–90%

Verify the exact numbers, drawdown type (static vs trailing), minimum trading days, news-trading and weekend-holding rules on your firm's dashboard — they vary and they change.

The math of passing

An 8% target at 0.5% risk and 2R targets needs roughly 8 net winning trades — entirely achievable over 20–40 trades with a modest edge. The target is rarely what fails people; the daily loss limit is. Treat the daily limit as the real game: your job is to survive long enough for the edge to show up.

Rules of engagement

  1. Risk 0.25–0.5% per trade. Slower, but the pass rate is what matters — not the pass date.
  2. Hard-stop your day at −1.5% or two losses. A 5% daily limit is a cliff edge, not a budget.
  3. Front-load caution near milestones. Up 6% of an 8% target? Cut risk to 0.25% — protect the progress.
  4. Trailing drawdown changes everything. If the floor follows your equity peak, bank progress calmly and never give back more than a third of an open winner; the tracker tab models both types.
  5. Don't trade the first and last 15 minutes of major news unless your strategy is specifically built for it — and check whether the firm bans it outright.
  6. Minimum trading days are a feature. They remove any reason to force trades. One good setup a day is plenty; zero is fine.

After you're funded

Nothing changes — that's the whole trick. The same 0.5% risk and daily stop that passed the evaluation keep the funded account. Withdraw profits on schedule, treat each payout as banked, and scale only through the firm's scaling plan rather than by raising risk.

Reality check: most evaluation attempts fail, fees are the firm's product, and past performance never guarantees future results. Only spend on evaluations what you can afford to lose entirely, and prove your strategy on demo first — it's free.

Trading psychology

Your strategy decides whether you can win; your psychology decides whether you actually collect. Every rule in the Risk tab exists because future-you, mid-drawdown, cannot be trusted without one. Full guide in docs/05-trading-psychology.md.

The five account-killers

  • Revenge trading — re-entering oversized right after a loss to "get it back." Antidote: the two-loss daily stop, obeyed mechanically.
  • FOMO entries — chasing a move that already left. If the plan's entry is gone, the trade is gone; there is a nearly identical setup next week.
  • Loss aversion — widening stops or removing them because closing "makes the loss real." The stop is the trade; touching it converts 1R into catastrophe.
  • Overconfidence after a streak — doubling risk because you're "in the zone." Streaks are variance; your edge didn't change. Risk % never rides a hot hand.
  • Recency bias — abandoning a positive-expectancy system after 3 losses. Judge systems on 30-trade blocks, never on the last session.

Protocols that actually work

  1. Decide everything before the trade. Entry, stop, target, size — written in the Planner while calm. Live-market you only executes; it never redesigns.
  2. Score process, not P/L. A losing trade that followed every rule is a good trade. Grade each journal entry A–D on execution; expectancy handles the money.
  3. The 10-minute rule. After any loss, hands off the platform for 10 minutes. Tilt has a half-life; give it one.
  4. Pre-commit your worst day. "Two losses or −1.5% and I close the platform" — said out loud before the session, it's a rule; invented during the session, it's a negotiation you'll lose.
  5. Trade smaller than feels meaningful. If a single loss changes your mood for the evening, halve the size until it doesn't. Execution quality is downstream of emotional stakes.
The evaluation is not testing whether you can read charts. It's testing whether you can follow your own rules for 30 consecutive trading days. Train that skill specifically.
